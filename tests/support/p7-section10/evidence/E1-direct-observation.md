**F-1 repair.** The structural witness (`verify` returning a caller-supplied `ok`), and the independent `ledger`/`cut`/witness, are gone.

- `CheckerContext.nativeEvidence` is OPAQUE evidence issued only by `bindNativeEvidence(store, {cutRevision})`: one authentic campaign, one valid exact cut, and **the one ledger over that cut**. It is authenticated by identity against a module-private registry; a literal, a spread clone, a prototype clone or a clone carrying a `verify` callback is `NATIVE_EVIDENCE_NOT_ISSUED`.
- The CHECKER calls the verifier itself, with the evidence, the event it re-read from its own ledger, and that ledger. Evidence for another ledger (an earlier cut, a rebuilt ledger, another campaign) is `LEDGER_NOT_THE_BOUND_ONE`.
- A cut must be an integer from 0 to the head: beyond the head, negative, fractional, `NaN`, infinite or non-numeric is `CUT_INVALID`, never clamped. The evidence is also pinned to the head's journal and state hashes (`JOURNAL_NOT_THE_PINNED_ONE`) and to a journal that has not been rewound (`JOURNAL_REWOUND`). The checker's canonical cut binds the time: evidence after it is `EVIDENCE_AFTER_CANONICAL_CUT`, an incomparable unit is `CLOCK_NOT_ONE_BASIS`.
- The verifier is installed into the E0 checker once, **write-once** (a second installation throws), by the native module when it loads. That keeps `proof.ts` free of any new import and the E0 boundary suite (`p7-section10-proof.test.ts`) **untouched and passing**: I considered importing the native module into `proof.ts` and rejected it, because that suite forbids it and the brief forbids changing earlier tests' expectations.

**F-2 repair.** Every fact the verifier relies on is now the exact write of its own strictly verified committed group, and the chain is event < authority < formation < seal ≤ cut:

- _Strict group and receipt layer_ (every fact shown at the cut, including the decision, premises and Role Registry binding the owner verifiers read): the group is well formed, has the identity asked for and is what the intent index returns; its receipt names this group and intent in this campaign, records the group's own rule and authority, and carries the canonical hash **recomputed from the returned group**. The fact is the group's write in key, owner, facet, value, unit, basis, secrecy, version (`expected_version + 1`) and provenance (group, intent, cause, rule).
- _Formation_: the Epistemic stage group (owner, facet, `EPISTEMIC_ACCESS_RULE`, canon-write authority, cause `transaction:<id>`), writing exactly the packet and the sealed provenance; its reads are exactly the event at its version, its dependencies exactly the event's group and the durable authority group; owner stage authority via `stageAuthorityViolation` and `observationTriggerViolation`.
- _Durable authority_: present, its own kernel group between the event and the formation, opened at the state version it read, repeating exactly what the seal repeats (transaction, cause, trigger, opening, request, context, publication labels).
- _Seal_: the transaction fact is the write of **its own** derived group (`transaction-group:<id>`), distinct from the formation and authority groups, committed strictly after the formation, reading the state at the opening version and the authority, depending on the authority and the formation; its stage record names that group, intent, receipt revision and canonical hash, the declared **realized reads with the event's version and committing group**, the group's writes at their committed versions, and the receipt's invariants. The owner's `observationTransactionViolation` is the final gate.

## 2. The gap this closes

E0's `DIRECT_OBSERVATION` branch could close the knowledge/latency obligation on a **derivable** receipt: the event is observation-earned, and the Epistemic owner's derivation would produce a packet. That is not the receipt the owner **committed**. E1 makes the route close only when the actual journal, at a named cut, shows the committed receipt. Without evidence the route fails closed (`NATIVE_RECEIPT_EVIDENCE_ABSENT`).

## 3. How it proves (it reads the real journal and reuses the owners' own functions)

For one event and character at one cut, it verifies, and refuses with a test-local diagnostic code otherwise:

1. The evidence was issued by the native verifier, is for the checker's own ledger, has a valid cut and the pinned journal; the event key and id are the **derived** ones (`observationEventId`) for this character at this basis; it is the **journal's own fact** at the cut.
2. **The owner's own verifier** `observationEventViolation(…, "VERIFICATION")` re-derives it from its retained direct-perception decision and premises, and `recipientBoundaryViolation` confirms the Role Registry binding names the character.
3. The actor packet and the sealed engine provenance are the exact writes of **one** stage group (`stageGroupId`), after the event and under the durable authority, in separated secrecy classes, equal to what **`deriveAccessProducts`** determines.
4. A sealed NOW-only **Temporal transaction** is the exact write of its own group, strictly after the formation, and names that exact stage, group, receipt (hash recomputed), realized reads and written versions, with no engaged object or boundary, one basis and unit throughout, and `TARGET_REACHED`.

It holds only the store's read methods (`head`, `getFact`, `lookupReceipt`, `committedGroup`, `committedGroupById`); the type has no commit, no close and no listing, and a static test pins that.

## 5. Mutation probes (refuting a superficial pass)

I broke each guard layer of the verifier, and the checker's bindings, on purpose (one whole layer per probe, not one term of it) and ran the E1 integration and boundary suites. **62 probes: 55 killed, 7 survived.**

How the number arose, step by step: a full run against the first repaired suite (31 tests) killed 50 of 62. I triaged the 12 survivors against the transaction schema and the earlier layers; five were reachable and got twins (the head-campaign pin, the observation-earned rule, a provenance written by a second real group, the explicit object/boundary guard through the engaged variants the schema does admit in a zero-span seal, and group identity); the five were re-run against the final 32-test suite and all five are killed (adding tests cannot un-kill an earlier kill). An earlier run of this probe harness reported crashed runs as kills; I discarded it, fixed the harness to read vitest's own JSON and to classify a crash or a syntactically broken mutant separately, and every number here is from the corrected harness.

The **seven survivors are redundant or unreachable layers, not gaps**, each with its reason:

- `FORMATION_AFTER_CUT`: unreachable. The strict view already hides the packet after the cut, before this guard is reached.
- `AUTHORITY_NOT_UNDER_ITS_DERIVED_IDENTITY` and `TRANSACTION_NOT_UNDER_ITS_DERIVED_IDENTITY`: behind the strict write layer. A fact whose provenance names another group is refused as `GROUP_DOES_NOT_WRITE_IT`; only a second committed group writing the same create-only key could reach them.
- `SEAL_NOT_ITS_OWN_GROUP`: behind the derived-identity check and the same strict layer.
- `OWNER_STAGE_AUTHORITY_REFUSED`: the owner's catch-all; each of its terms (identity, cause, dependency on the authority group, target) is checked earlier by an explicit guard.
- `TRANSACTION_NOT_TARGET_REACHED_CLOSED`: implied by the transaction schema once no object is engaged and no condition is requested, where `TARGET_REACHED` is the only stop reason it admits.
- `OWNER_TRANSACTION_REFUSED`: the owner's own statement of what an observation transaction is, kept deliberately as the final gate over the explicit guards.

The explicit "no engaged object or boundary" guard that BLUE asked to preserve is **not** one of the survivors any more: it is killed by two twins that the schema admits (a `DUE_UNRESOLVED` engaged object, and a `RETIRED` object with a crossed boundary). These runs are the Builder's, and BLUE has not re-executed them.

## 7. Limits, stated plainly

- **T1 mechanism only.** The referee decision is the shipped scripted-host seam, not genuine LLM reasoning, an actual client or retained production storage. Those are UNRUN.
- **The forged fixtures are read-only overlays** on a real store and are labelled as forged. The _self-consistent_ forgeries rewrite the group and recompute its receipt hash, so that only the layer under test can refuse; the _inconsistent_ ones are refused by the strict group/receipt layer. BLUE's own boundary applies: this is not generic tamper-proofing against the replacement of the whole trusted store; a wholesale, self-consistent substitute of the entire journal is, by construction, indistinguishable from the journal.
- **The route is not a certified member.** Health ability, first option and history stay at their refusing defaults. The E0 member checker still queries the ability and first-option authorities with `modality: "SPEECH"` for every route (my E0 limitation, already excluded by the brief); E1 neither uses nor extends it, and does not infer sight.
- **Not done:** original in-X recipient-stage evidence, any interruption, S4, all-enrolled acceptance, the scheduled (`NON_OBSERVATION_ACCESS`) route's native proof, and any production path.
- **Historical support is deliberately narrow.** A fact is visible at a cut only when the group that committed its **current version** is strictly valid and committed at or before the cut. That is exact for create-only receipt records (event, packet, provenance, authority, seal) and for source versions that have not moved since; a fact whose current version was committed after the cut is hidden, never reconstructed, and a required source that cannot be read refuses. It is not a general historical reader.
- **The canonical time bound is the checker's.** The journal cut is a revision. The canonical time it corresponds to is bound only through the checker's own `cut` (the access time may not lie after it; an incomparable unit refuses). The temporal state at a past cut is a moving fact and is not reconstructed, in line with the narrow-history rule above.
- **A module-level slot exists in `proof.ts`** (the write-once native verifier). It holds a function and no data; a second installation throws. It is the cost of leaving the E0 import boundary untouched.
- **The mutation counts above are the Builder's own runs;** BLUE has not re-executed them.
