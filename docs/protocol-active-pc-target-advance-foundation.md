# Trusted Separately Player-Authorized Active-PC Target-Only Temporal Advance Gateway Foundation

Bounded slice built on the existing Temporal kernel (`docs/temporal-foundation.md`), the real shipped owner stage providers (including GIP's own sunset expiry, `docs/gip-governance-instrument-sunset-foundation.md`) and the protocol package's earlier gateways (`docs/protocol-pc-transfer-advance-foundation.md`, `docs/protocol-null-pc-ooc-advance-foundation.md`, `docs/protocol-null-pc-ooc-held-continuation-foundation.md`). **When a campaign has one active Player Character that is stationary, trusted server-side code that holds the player's separate, explicit choice of a canonical target can bind the PC, that exact target and the choice's durable transaction identity and provenance into a trusted context; an untrusted host can then invoke one allowlisted operation that names nothing, and the existing `TemporalCoordinator.advance` moves the campaign's canonical time to that target over the real owner stages.** When a due owner stage is genuinely owed — for example GIP's `gip-expire-instrument-at-sunset` for an Instrument whose exact `sunset_condition` falls inside the interval — the kernel runs it, the owner alone writes its result, and a later authoritative owner read shows it. A truthful kernel hold (`DEPENDENCY_UNRESOLVED`) can be continued only by a NEW, separately confirmed choice for exactly that lineage. It is a bounded technical operator/protocol ingress for temporal progression, **not** general play, a natural-language wait, an in-world waiting action, an action-cost or duration determination, a general continuation framework or a source of PC omniscience.

## The trust boundary and what "separately player-authorized" means here

1. **The player's choice is a precondition of the binder call, not a field.** The trusted service calls a binder only after the player has made a genuine, separate choice — an exact target in the campaign's actual canonical unit, confirmed apart from every earlier choice. It passes the one active `pc_id`, the exact `target` (an existing `CanonicalTimeSchema` value) and the choice's durable identity and authored provenance — exactly the existing `ResolutionRequestSchema` fields the trusted side alone may state: `transaction_id`, `cause`, `trigger { kind, ref }`, `context { role, permission }` and `publication { basis, secrecy }` — validated by the existing strict schema (`ResolutionRequestSchema.pick`). The gateway never states or defaults the role or permission. A binding is inert if the target is not a `CanonicalTimeSchema` value, if the transaction is not stated under exactly `role.player` + `permission.player_choice.commit` (so a `role.player_character` or `role.archivist` context, and a `continues` or `requested` field, are inert), if identity or publication material is malformed, or if an identity is the PC's own movement identity (a movement is not a progression instruction). The held binder additionally requires the held lineage to be exactly `{ from_transaction, target }`, and the new identity to differ from the held transaction.
2. **No time is invented.** The target is the trusted side's attestation of what the player chose; no calendar, unit, duration, date parser, default or inferred horizon exists. A target in another unit or before the canonical time is the kernel's refusal. The operation is never a natural-language wait, an in-world waiting action, or an action-cost or duration determination.
3. **Identity is the trusted side's, stable across retries and reopen.** The transaction identity and provenance come from the context and are retained by the caller; the gateway derives nothing from a random value, clock, counter or revision and never re-identifies a retry.
4. **Not claimed.** The gateway does not verify a human, a session, an identity or that a choice interaction took place; login, credentials, sessions, accounts, tenants and any confirmation UX are not modelled, not implemented and not claimed here. Calling a binder is the trusted side's attestation, nothing more. The local operator command (`docs/local-operator-command-reopen-foundation.md`) verifies only an operating-system user and a typed console challenge, and says so.
5. **Trusted context is not canon.** Every invocation re-reads the operative `role.player_character` binding, the PC's custody and movement state and the Temporal state, and then the kernel runs its own admission. A context cannot turn a stale, foreign or fabricated state into a lawful one, and cannot name a GIP result: there is no field anywhere through which `expired`, a due flag or a stage can enter.
6. **One refusal, one unknown.** Every failure — over-claiming request, forged or incomplete context, an absent or other PC, a PC that is not stationary, a held remainder against an initial choice, a stale/foreign/changed-target hold, a wrong unit or past target, an unregistered GIP sunset, a conflicting replay, any kernel refusal — is `PC_TARGET_ADVANCE_UNAVAILABLE` ("no advance is available") with no cause, owner, key, identity, target, stop reason or detail, and nothing is written. A failure after anything may have committed (an `UNKNOWN_OUTCOME`, a poisoned or closed store, or any change in the store head since the call began) is the distinct `PC_TARGET_ADVANCE_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` (RC-16), never "safely no advancement".
7. **Minimum receipt.** Success is exactly `{ status: "PC_TARGET_ADVANCE_COMMITTED" }`: no revision, hash, key, owner, replay flag, time, stop reason, remainder, event, recipient, owner result or attainment. It is identical for a first call and a retry, for the initial and the held branch, and whether the kernel reached the target, crossed nothing or committed another held remainder. Held is not completion, and the host cannot tell. It is a **transaction status**: it never reports that the target was attained, that an Instrument expired, or that the PC saw or learned anything.
8. **Isolation.** A context is bound to one campaign object and one PC, frozen and opaque; the gateway constructs its coordinator from that campaign's own store. Two campaigns with identical identifiers cannot cross. This is bounded campaign-isolation evidence for this operation, **not** an authentication or session isolation claim.

## The active-PC rule: stationary, with nothing of its own in flight (F-014B §10 is not weakened)

The W3-CP3 No-Active-PC overlay of F-014B is exclusive to a campaign with `role.player_character = NULL`; the NULL-PC gateways stay inert while a PC is active and this gateway borrows nothing from them. While a PC **is** active, F-014B §10's actionable-knowledge interruption applies. This engine has no PC actionable-knowledge-interruption evaluator, and every Temporal object it can register declares `creates_epistemic_interruption: false` (the schema admits only that literal). The gateway therefore does **not** treat "stationary custody" as making every in-world activity freely interruptible, and it does not invent a `true` branch: it refuses unless it can positively establish, from committed owner state read fresh by identity, that the PC's own commitments leave nothing to interrupt:

- the bound PC is the operative `role.player_character` binding (read first, before any other fact); and
- World-State is the present authority over the PC's locus, with a stationary locus; and
- **either** the PC has no movement of any kind (`STATIONARY_AT_ORIGIN`: no movement commitment-of-record, no PC-owned Movement Service Request, no Logistics commitment or transfer, no registered transfer object), **or** its one single-leg movement is fully settled (`DESTINATION_COMMITTED`: the destination committed by World-State, the Logistics transfer `RETIRED`, the commitment-of-record and request present); and
- the PC's transfer object holds no pending earned boundary and the active boundary index names none of it.

Every other state — a PC in transfer, a willingness, request or Logistics commitment not yet resolved, a destination commit awaiting retirement, a missing custody or locus, anything the check does not positively recognise — is the one refusal and nothing is read further or written. The demonstrated positive case is specifically a fully supported owner-registered interval with no PC-actionable interruption. The check is evaluated on every fresh (non-replay) invocation; a replay of an already committed identity writes nothing and re-proves only that the bound PC is still the operative one (a later lawful relocation does not turn a committed status into "unavailable").

## The literal Temporal request

The operation builds exactly one `ResolutionRequestSchema` value and nothing else:

```
transaction_id, cause, trigger, context, publication   ← from the trusted context (verbatim, never defaulted)
continues: <null | held.from_transaction>              ← null for the initial binder; exactly the persisted continuation for the held binder
requested: { target, condition: null }                 ← initial: the player's exact target, target-only (condition mandatory-nullable, null)
requested: <the held transaction's recorded request>   ← held: verbatim (never a new target, condition, amount or duration)
```

It drives the request through the **existing** `TemporalCoordinator.advance` over the real shipped owner stage providers — the same fifteen the NULL-PC gateways register (a conformance test pins the three lists to each other), with the existing Population barrier evaluator — so the kernel alone opens the transaction's authority before any owner stage, selects what is due from the active boundary index, resolves each declared dependency closure through its owner path in F-017 order, and records the transaction, the new canonical time and the stop/continuation in its own groups. A committed-but-unregistered GIP sunset refuses the whole request (`ENROLLMENT_PENDING`) before anything is opened; a legitimately empty due index goes to the kernel unchanged and performs F-014B §6's exact lazy carry with no owner effect (the presence or absence of due work is never a host-visible difference).

## GIP: the real owner result, with no verdict laundering

GIP's `gip-expire-instrument-at-sunset` provider runs if and only if the kernel finds the Instrument's earned sunset boundary due by the target (`t1 ≥ tS`): GIP reads exactly its two committed Office and Scope Binding sources and the current Instrument, and alone writes one updated `gip/instrument/<instrument_id>` value with `lifecycle_status = expired`, preserving the source and validity dimensions. Neither the host, the trusted context, the binder nor this module authors `expired`, a due flag or a stage. The witness is read back from GIP's own authoritative read (`GipAdapter.governanceInstrument`), not from the receipt: a target strictly before `tS` leaves the same Instrument `active` and its sunset enrolled while canonical time is lawfully carried; at or after `tS` it expires exactly once and later advances never expire it again. Expiry is a GIP lifecycle derivation, **not** knowledge: the receipt, the console and the ledger say nothing about it, and no PC receipt of the event is inferred (F-014D / I-001 boundaries).

## The held branch

When the first choice's transaction committed a truthful `DEPENDENCY_UNRESOLVED` stop (an owner stage refused, a required source absent, a provider missing) and the kernel holds an unresolved remainder, the same operation — reached through the **held** binder — lets the player choose, anew, to continue exactly that held work. It proceeds only when the Temporal state still holds a continuation whose `from_transaction` is the bound held transaction, and the sealed lineage (the held transaction and every transaction it continued, back to its initial one) stopped `DEPENDENCY_UNRESOLVED` having requested exactly the bound target and **no condition**. A stop-condition lineage (the PC stop-at-delivery advance), a different target, a missing transaction, a stale or consumed hold and a foreign hold are the one refusal. `continues` is the held `from_transaction` and `requested` is the held record's request verbatim. Nothing is cancelled, abandoned, superseded, resumed automatically or advanced silently: an initial choice while a hold stands is refused (the kernel's `CONTINUATION_HELD` guard stays the final authority), and a persisting fault commits another truthful hold that a third choice resumes. If the held path cannot be supported for a given state, the operation refuses that branch rather than report completion.

## Recovery

The same request with the same transaction identity (and, for the held branch, the same lineage) — with the same context or a freshly bound equivalent, in the same process or after save → close → load — is the recovery path (RC-16):

- A transaction that already committed under that identity is replayed by the kernel against the same literal request: the status is the same, nothing is written, no second clock advance occurs, and committed stages are adopted by identity and never recomputed. An identity replays **only itself, as the branch its binder carries**: an initial record is not replayed through the held binder, nor a continuation through the initial one, and the same identity with a different target, cause, trigger, context or publication is the kernel's conflict and is refused; the committed record is never amended.
- A partial run (a crash or lost reply after the opening authority, GIP's stage or the sealed record) leaves the boundary pending and any hold intact until the transaction commits, so the re-presented request adopts every committed stage and commits the rest, converging byte-identically on the uninterrupted run with no second expiry.
- A lost reply or an ambiguous commit is `PC_TARGET_ADVANCE_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`. A failure that committed nothing is the plain refusal.

## Residuals

- Whether the player's choice actually occurred, and whether the target the trusted side attests is the one the player chose, is outside this slice; a binder's caller owns that precondition and must retain the target, the held lineage, the transaction identity and its provenance across retries and reopen. The local operator command retains them in its owner-only ledger; authentication, session binding and a real confirmation surface remain future, separately authorised work.
- **Choice left to engineering:** one allowlisted operation with two trusted-side binders (initial and held), rather than two operations, so the host surface stays a single name that cannot say which branch it is; the NULL-PC pair, by contrast, is two operations. The fifteen-provider registry is duplicated from the NULL-PC gateways (sharing would edit them); a conformance test fails if the lists drift.
- **Choice left to engineering:** "stationary" is the positively-recognised set above (never-moved, or the settled single leg). A PC settled at its destination is treated as stationary because World-State is then the one authority over a stationary locus and every movement record is resolved; if BLUE reads the brief's "committed stationary custody" as origin-only, the `DESTINATION_COMMITTED` arm is the one line to remove.
- **Choice left to engineering:** the shipped provider set includes the Epistemic-packet, KTI result-close and PC-capability-close providers the NULL-PC gateways register. Their objects declare no epistemic interruption and the Epistemic and KTI owners address named non-PC recipients in this engine, so none creates a PC-actionable interruption; they are not separately exercised here, and the live-producer question stays with the future interruption evaluator.
- The mechanism is demonstrated end to end with GIP's sunset expiry (the positive witness and its before-boundary contrast), an owner stage refused through the store (a genuine kernel hold and its recovery), and the settled single-leg PC (stationary at its destination). The Logistics chain is exercised only as the PC's own already-settled movement.
- A PC that is relocated or otherwise leaves the stationary state between a commit and a retry still replays the committed status; one that stops being the operative PC makes the retry the plain refusal. The committed transaction is unaffected.
- Local success is not provider or recovery acceptance (IA-07 BLOCKED). No register evidence was added; whole-row status remains separately governed.
