## Temporal enrollment (forty-second slice)

Authority: the Forty-second implementation brief (Blue Hard Preflight, native pin `2026-10-08T06:07:17.605Z`) on official `main` `d8307d513493ea50a2bbc583f1fb4a99ddfcaed1`, under the same F-027 v1.0 / F-027-CD1 lock (`spec/f027/spec-lock.json`, unchanged), F-014B Turnless Temporal §§3, 6, 13, 18-20 (registration independent of resolution; an earned one-object boundary), WS-010 Logistics §§3.1-3.4, 9, 10.1, 10.4, 11, 20, 23 and I-001 §§2, 4, 8, 14, 17. Integration engineering only: no new owner, fact kind, schema, host field, status, source pin, gate or time rule.

After the four-step dispatch the gateway performs a fifth publication: it registers the SAME transfer as the one object the existing `logisticsTemporalObject` derives from the committed Logistics commitment and the dispatched transfer, through the existing `TemporalCoordinator.registerObject` (`object_id` `logisticsObjectId(request_id)`, Logistics-owned process, `EXACT-PARTITION`, `last_resolved_basis` = the dispatch basis, its single `next_boundary` at the already-declared `delivery.at`, and the three declared stages Logistics delivery → World-State `entityTransitionDeclaration` → Logistics retirement). The object is never reconstructed, shortened or authored here, and the existing RC-10 registration admission (`logisticsObjectViolation`) re-judges it. The group is `pc-relocation-group:temporal:<pc>` / `pc-relocation-intent:temporal:<pc>`, derived from the PC identity alone. The only facts added beyond the earlier four are the kernel's own object and its boundary-index entry.

- **Registration is not resolution.** It stages and resolves nothing, advances no time, creates no presentation event, and the PC stays `IN_TRANSFER` under Logistics with no delivery and no destination until a separately authorised `TemporalCoordinator.advance` runs the existing delivery → destination → retirement chain.
- **Idempotence.** A registration that already committed (retry, lost reply, reopened campaign, or a boundary since resolved) is replayed by its immutable group/intent identity from the store's committed group: no second object, boundary, clock change or delivery, and a resolved boundary is neither re-created nor re-run. A request that finds the transfer committed but unregistered registers it.
- **Fail closed.** The transfer is committed once the dispatch commits and is never rolled back. Anything short of a complete registration after that point (a mismatched or stale commitment or transfer, a rival or differently-registered object, a kernel refusal, an ambiguous commit) is `RELOCATION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never `RELOCATION_UNAVAILABLE`, so a committed movement is never misreported as safely absent; no detail is added and nothing is repaired or defaulted. Presenting the same request reconciles by identity.
- **Host surface unchanged.** The request is still `{ operation: "commit_confirmed_pc_relocation" }`, the success is still exactly `{ status: "RELOCATION_COMMITTED" }` (in transfer, not arrived), and every refusal is still the one refusal.

## The trust boundary and what "confirmed" means here

1. **Confirmation is a precondition of the binder call, not a field.** The trusted service calls the binder only after the player has confirmed this exact destination by the service's own means. The `choice` it passes is the exact existing `PcMovementCommitInput` — `{ pc_id, destination: { world_id, locus_id }, authored_under: { role: "role.player", permission: "permission.player_choice.commit" } }` — validated by the existing strict schema. The gateway never states the authority itself and never defaults it: a choice not stated under exactly that role and permission yields an inert handle. There is no `confirmed` flag, role claim, LLM wording or host assertion that can stand for the confirmation.
2. **Not claimed.** The gateway does not verify a human, a session, an identity or that a confirmation interaction took place; login, credentials, sessions, accounts, tenants and the confirmation UI are not modelled, not implemented and not claimed. Calling the binder is the trusted side's attestation, nothing more. A deployment that cannot truthfully make that attestation must not call it.
3. **Authority is the context, never the request.** The campaign, PC, destination, authored authority, declared delivery boundary and publication material (`cause`, `basis`, `secrecy`, which F-027 defines no vocabulary for and which are therefore authored by the trusted side and never defaulted) come from the context. The host request carries only the operation name.
4. **Trusted context is not canon.** Every invocation re-runs the existing owner admission: the PC must be the active binding's PC at the pinned version, its stationary custody must hold as the one authority, the destination must be an established distinct locus of its world, and one slot per PC/entity applies at every layer. A wrong PC, stale binding or custody, mismatched or unestablished destination, rival locus, existing transfer or second move is refused by the owners, not by gateway logic.
5. **One refusal, one unknown.** Every failure — over-claiming request, forged or incomplete context, any owner refusal — is `RELOCATION_UNAVAILABLE` ("no relocation is available") with no cause, owner, key, identity or detail. An ambiguous infrastructure outcome is the distinct `RELOCATION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` (RC-16), not success and not failure.
6. **Minimum receipt.** Success is exactly `{ status: "RELOCATION_COMMITTED" }`: no revision, hash, key, owner, replay flag, locus or arrival. It is identical for a first call and a retry, so it is not a new player-facing consequence channel; results reach the player only through the existing IM-13 → IM-14 path.
7. **Isolation.** A context is bound to one campaign object; the gateway constructs its Logistics adapter from that campaign's own store. Two campaigns with identical PC, world, locus and group identifiers cannot cross. This is bounded campaign-isolation evidence for this operation, **not** an authentication or session isolation claim.

## The sequence

The operation commits, in order, each under its own existing owner and unchanged in meaning:

1. `CanonicalCampaign.commitPcVoluntaryMovement` — the PC-side commitment-of-record (`pc_movement_commitment`).
2. `CanonicalCampaign.establishPcMovementRequest` — the PC-owned Movement Service Request (`pc_movement_request`).
3. `LogisticsAdapter.commitMovement` — the single-leg Logistics commitment at the trusted side's explicitly declared `delivery_at`.
4. `LogisticsAdapter.dispatch` — the transfer; World-State's stationary custody yields atomically with this commit and Logistics is the sole in-transfer locus.

Group and intent identities are `pc-relocation-group:<step>:<pc>` and `pc-relocation-intent:<step>:<pc>`, derived from the canonical PC identity alone (technical encodings; no clock, counter, random value or host-supplied idempotency).

5. `TemporalCoordinator.registerObject` — the transfer's one object under its one declared delivery boundary (see "Temporal enrollment"; forty-second slice).

**The gateway advances no time, resolves no boundary and promises no arrival.** Resolving the declared boundary remains the existing Temporal machinery, driven by the trusted side by its normal means; the existing Logistics delivery → World-State destination commit → Logistics retirement chain is the only arrival path. Until then the PC is `IN_TRANSFER` with Logistics as the one authority.

## Recovery

Presenting the same request again — with the same context or a freshly bound equivalent — is the recovery path. Every committed step replays (`replayed`), the remaining steps commit, and the run converges on the uninterrupted one; after a commit a retry returns the same status and writes nothing, in transfer or after arrival. A lost reply or crash at any of the five commits (including after dispatch and before registration) is `RELOCATION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and reconciles by identity, including across save → close → load. A partial sequence (for example, a declared boundary the Logistics commitment refuses after the two PC-side steps committed) is recoverable only through the existing validated current state: the PC stays stationary at its origin under World-State, nothing is rolled back, and the same PC relocates once the lawful boundary is declared. A changed input (a different boundary after Logistics committed) is a conflict, never a silent reuse.

## Residuals

- Whether a confirmation actually occurred is outside this slice; the binder's caller owns that precondition. Real authentication, session binding and a confirmation surface remain future, separately authorised work.
- The gateway enrolls the transfer but never advances time; a trusted caller must advance by the existing, separately authorised means for arrival. A caller that registered the same transfer's object by another identity before the gateway ran leaves the gateway's request unknown (it will not duplicate or repair the object).
- Exactly one journey per PC under the current one-slot constraints; a second relocation is refused by the existing owners.
- A declared boundary the Logistics commitment refuses is only discovered after the PC-side steps commit; the committed willingness and request stand (no invented rollback) and the retry converges.
- Local success is not provider or recovery acceptance (IA-07 BLOCKED). No register evidence was added; whole-row status remains separately governed.
