# Single-Leg Logistics Transfer & Destination Handoff Backbone Foundation

Eighth bounded slice, built on the Campaign Continuity Foundation (`docs/campaign-package.md`), the Canonical World Identity & Owner Foundation (`docs/canonical-world-foundation.md`), the Turnless Temporal & Resolution Transaction Foundation (`docs/temporal-foundation.md`, including its durable pre-stage transaction authority), the Population (`docs/population-foundation.md`), Production (`docs/production-foundation.md`), Economy (`docs/economy-foundation.md`) and World-State Entity Continuity / Transition Close (`docs/entity-continuity-foundation.md`) foundations. An **already-accepted fallback physical entity** can now **move once, from the stationary locus World-State holds to another established locus of its world**: an authorized Movement Service Request exists; **Logistics establishes the minimum lawful movement-service commitment**; the entity **dispatches**; **Logistics is the sole authoritative current movement locus while the transfer is active**; Logistics **establishes delivery completion**; **World-State commits the destination through the existing ECT-01 / Entity Transition Close seam**; and **only after that destination commit** does Logistics retire the transfer and release the commitment. The same entity continues throughout, is never duplicated, and its sealed accepted history is never rewritten. After save, close and load — at every durable cut of the lifecycle — nothing is duplicated and exactly one authority answers "where is this entity?".

Governing lock: `spec/f027/spec-lock.json` (F-027 v1.0). Sources: WS-010 Logistics (Working Operational Draft v1 / W3-OD-L; lock pin `2026-09-25T02:18:10.619Z`) §2.1, §2.2, §3.1, §3.2, §3.4, §4.1, §9, §10.1, §10.2, §10.4, §11, §20, §23, §26, §27; F-016 v0.4 ECT-01 and the cause-owner / entity-commit split (pin `2026-09-25T13:45:28.162Z`); F-017 v0.3 (conformance pin `2026-09-25T23:42:27.802Z`), the §23 completion edge pair only, as a lawful NOW order. **CAL-01 (APPROXIMATE-PARTITION) remains UNCALIBRATED and is not used: every proof is exact. No travel time, duration, capacity, throughput, route or priority is defined anywhere: the delivery boundary is an explicit declared input.** Every other F-027 open gate is unchanged.

This is a **foundation proof**, not a Logistics simulator. All fixture data is synthetic (`packages/testkit/src/logistics-fixtures.ts`): the **requester** of the movement is a clearly synthetic testkit party registered through the existing extension seam (which cannot write a canonical, kernel, Population, Production, Economy, World-State or Logistics key space); it is the interface envelope's owner (WS-010 §3.1) and never becomes the owner of Logistics state, of the entity or of its location. Logistics and the World-State custodian are real and live in `@immortalis/engine`.

## One canon: how it reuses IM-02 / IM-03 / IM-11 / IM-12 / IM-17

Nothing new is persisted outside the existing campaign package. Logistics facts are ordinary `OwnerFact`s written through `store.commit`, so the existing validation, expected-version checks, RC-16 receipts, idempotent replay, unknown-outcome reconciliation, hash-chained journal and package/load integrity are the ones in force. **The package format is unchanged (still format version 1); there is no second journal, scheduler, location ledger, carrier/capacity ledger, sidecar save path, polling loop or per-object refresh.** The temporal coordinator was not changed (a conformance test asserts it never names Logistics). The only edits to predecessor engine code are additive wiring: the registry gains the three kinds, `identityMismatch` handles them, `canonicalAdmission` dispatches the Logistics owner, object registration also asks the Logistics rule, `CanonicalCampaign.open`/`verify` also run the Logistics verifier, and the **existing entity-transition seam** gains two narrow seams (below).

## Where is this entity? One authority at every stage

The accepted entity is a **sealed, create-only** fact and is never touched. Its locality stays the historical origin. The current locus is answered by one rule (`resolveEntityLocus`):

| Durable state                                                          | Phase                                      | Authority                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| no commitment, or committed and not dispatched                         | `STATIONARY_AT_ORIGIN`                     | World-State's stationary locus (the accepted locality)                                                              |
| transfer `IN_TRANSFER`, with or without Logistics' delivery result     | `IN_TRANSFER`                              | **Logistics' movement locus**; the entity is nowhere stationary. Logistics' arrival is **not** destination truth.   |
| World-State's transition committed, transfer not yet retired           | `DESTINATION_COMMITTED_RETIREMENT_PENDING` | World-State's destination. Logistics' movement locus has **lapsed** with the destination commit; it is not a rival. |
| transition committed, transfer `RETIRED` (or no Logistics involvement) | `DESTINATION_COMMITTED`                    | World-State's destination                                                                                           |

Authority passes atomically with the destination commit, so there is never a state with two. Logistics cannot retire early: the retirement stage reads World-State's committed transition for _that very delivery_ AS-OF-NOW, so it is a dependent of the destination commit, and admission re-derives it from the committed chain (a raw retirement of the exact lawful values before the commit is refused). The window between the destination commit and the retirement is a real durable cut (a crash there is recovered by adoption), and a test asserts exactly one authority at it.

## The chain: Logistics completion → destination commit → Logistics retirement

WS-010 §10.4 / §23 and F-017's "completion edge pair" are a lawful order, not a cycle, because they are three stages over three distinct produced facts inside **one** engaged object and boundary: stage 1 (Logistics) produces the delivery result from the request it serves; stage 2 is World-State's existing `world-state-commit-entity-transition`, declared through the existing `entityTransitionDeclaration`, reading the delivery result as its causing result; stage 3 (Logistics) reads the delivery result and the committed transition and advances the commitment and transfer together. The coordinator resolves them in dependency order inside one transaction; each commits as its owner's group under its StageKey-derived identity and the transaction's durable authority. A stage committed before a crash is adopted by identity, never recomputed.

## Reuse of the ECT-01 seam: two additive seams in the entity-transition modules

The destination handoff is World-State's own, unchanged ECT-01 mechanism. Reuse needed exactly two additions there, both fail-closed:

1. **A registered cause owner (as the seventh slice's doc anticipated).** The causing result may now also be Logistics' registered `logistics_delivery` fact (at the key derived from the entity it moves, owner `logistics`, source naming itself). Its engagement is the Logistics transfer object (`entityTransitionEngagement`), and a standalone entity-transition object for a Logistics delivery is refused: a stage is declared by exactly one engaged object. The delivery's `established_at` is the transfer's dispatch basis (the object's last resolved basis) and `occurs_at` the declared delivery boundary, so the existing "takes effect after it was established" rule holds unchanged.
2. **The movement guard.** Once Logistics has committed to move an entity, World-State commits that entity's destination **only** as the consequence of that commitment's delivery result. A foreign causing result for such an entity earns no object at registration and is refused at admission and on load, so World-State and Logistics can never be made to claim rival current loci. (A foreign cause registered _before_ the commitment simply stays unresolved and fail-closed.)

## Judgment calls and open residuals (none resolves an open gate)

- **Declared delivery boundary.** The sources give no travel-time rule and no calibration exists, so the commitment carries an explicit, caller-declared delivery boundary (as Production's completion boundary is), never a Logistics default.
- **One movement slot per entity; one transition per entity.** All three Logistics key spaces are keyed by the entity, so a competing request, a duplicate, or a later movement of the same entity is a version conflict, never a sibling truth. The seventh slice's one-transition-per-entity limitation is inherited and **fails closed**: a movement of an entity that already changed locus (by this movement or any other cause) is refused at commitment; no chained transition, successor protocol or second location mechanism is invented. A second later journey is outside this tranche.
- **Request after dispatch.** Stage 1 reads the request AS-OF-NOW, so a requester that advances its own request fact after dispatch leaves the delivery uncredited and the entity in transfer (fail closed, tested). WS-010 §9 says cancellation after dispatch must resolve as diversion/return or termination-in-place; those are **not implemented**, so there is deliberately no cancellation path. (Reading the request is what keeps stage 1 free of a cycle with stage 3, which advances the commitment and transfer.)
- **Registration is a separate step, as in Production.** Dispatch and the registration of the transfer's temporal object are separate publications. Between them (a real durable cut, tested) the transfer is in transfer with no boundary; the registration is idempotent by identity, and nothing is delivered until it is made.
- **Request authorization.** "Authorized" means the requester owner's own committed request (a priority/authority reference is unrepresentable). Logistics adjudicates no authority, willingness, affordability or access (WS-010 §15).
- **Commitment state.** Only `COMMITTED`/`RELEASED` exist; cancellation before dispatch (WS-010 §9) is not implemented.
- **Basis/secrecy.** The commitment and dispatch carry their publication's authored labels; the delivery result inherits the request's and the retirement the commitment's, never defaulted.
- **Stage authority.** Both Logistics stages obey the repaired IM-02 durable transaction authority through the one shared rule; a forged-cause stage with the right identity publishes nothing (tested).
- **Cost.** The advance path reads the engaged chain by identity: the request, the entity, the world and destination locus, and the movement's own facts (and the object and state). `verifyLogisticsIntegrity` (like the other verifiers) lists all facts once per open — a diagnostic path (CAL-06), not the advance path.

Route finding, pathfinding, Route Families, hubs, transshipment, multi-leg travel, rerouting, competing carriers, contention arbitration, priority selection, capacity or throughput, travel-time calibration; partial delivery, cargo subdivision, loss, spoilage, damage, destruction, theft, seizure, abandonment, diversion, return, termination-in-place, cancellation or withdrawal; recurring and continuous-flow commitments; agentive carriers, labor-bearing movement, passengers, escorts; Population migration, Military logistics, Health transport; KTI and Epistemic consequences; Actor/ACS decisions; Economy prices, markets, money, credit; a generic inventory/asset or per-object movement subsystem; a second or later movement of one entity; provider adoption, deployment, managed database recovery, migration and any PF-6/F-028 activation.
