## Neutral contract (host-independent)

The operations are typed requests over the existing shipped operations; MCP (Streamable HTTP, spec 2025-06-18) is only the first transport. Another qualified host needs an adapter that can (1) authenticate, (2) bind exact authored requests to their supported operations, and (3) present complete readback and genuine missing-information questions. Supported chat requires no human-typed approval reply. A tool call still does not prove original human wording.

## Chat-requested ORIGINAL setting (BLUE v5.2-A1, 24 October)

A deliberately small predecessor of conversational campaign creation. When the player **actually chooses** to write their own invented place, an authenticated chat host can propose it as one strict document and the player confirms it themselves; the SHIPPED `establish-locality` path and the World-State owners (`establishWorld` → `establishLocus` → `establishSourceCondition`) are unchanged and decide everything. It is a host/setup mechanism, **not** world generation, character creation, activation, placement or the start of play, and it advertises none of them. The same service may point at a fresh, empty campaign slot (the configured campaign directory need not exist yet); allocation of further campaigns or accounts is outside it.

- **Tools** (player scope only; a referee-only grant cannot list or call them): `propose_original_setting` takes exactly the `immortalis-original-locality-setup` v1 document (`world`, `locus`, `source`; the World Profile restricted to the `ORIGINAL` reference mode; the source `{aspect, statement}` access facts and the positive-integer quantity). The engine's own `LocalitySetupSchema` is the authority and is strict: an extra field (a confirmation, a campaign, a path, an owner), a mismatched identity, another reference mode or the optional acoustic profile this command does not admit is refused before anything starts. The campaign, directories, credentials and permissions come only from the service's configuration; the fictional world/locus/source identifiers are data. `original_setting_status` is read-only and settles nothing. `resume_original_setting` takes no argument. Setup needs neither the chat scenario file nor a character.
- **Confirmation is the human's, and complete.** The model starts the shipped command; the player confirms through the client's own elicitation (or the disclosed development fallback page), exactly as for every other action, and no tool argument can supply the reply. For this setting the confirmation is **never truncated**: an ordinary proposal's confirmation keeps its last 6,000 characters, but the setting's is the engine's whole text, in order, delivered in as many consecutive elicitation messages as the channel needs (`[Part i of n]`; an earlier part only asks the player to read on and confirms nothing, anything but "accept" withdraws; the last part carries the challenge phrase). If the transcript no longer holds the whole of it, the confirmation is declined before it can be given. A setting with more separate entries than the transcript can hold is refused at the proposal, before anything is started. The model is never shown the challenge phrase.
- **What is retained before dispatch** (the existing pending-operation record): the exact proposed document, its SHA-256 and the setup identity (the campaign-bound hash `establish-locality` itself derives). This record is **not** consent and is not a second save; the existing post-confirmation **locality pin** (first-writer-wins, created only after the human confirmed) remains decisive. The temporary input file is removed after the run; the retained document is what can be resumed.
- **Owner evidence about THAT document** (`localityOutcome` in `apps/phone/src/locality-evidence.ts`, the adapter's own module: `packages/operator`, the owners and the frozen specification are unchanged; read-only: it replays nothing, reclaims no lock and asks nobody): the retained pin, the exact owner publications by their original identities and a fresh owner-verified open. Distinguished: **no effect** (no identity was ever retained and no package exists; or the campaign's pin is another setup and none of this setup's publications exist) – proven, not unknown; **resumable** (this setup's identity is retained and the package is absent, empty or holds a prefix of world → locus → source); **complete** (identity, all three publications and the fresh open agree with the document, read back as the allowlisted, IMMUTABLE authored setting only: world, locus, source key, material class and the authored established quantity. The owner's current remainder, deliveries and any later owner state are verified internally and **never exported** to the player: that is world truth with no access basis here, and gameplay information reaches the player only through the earned Glance); **blocked** (a malformed or foreign identity file, a package that is neither empty nor complete, a conflicting or unpinned commit, a verification mismatch – engineering inspects it; nothing is adopted, deleted or replaced); **unavailable** (a live or stale lock, an unreadable package – neither done nor not done; the lock is never reclaimed).
- **No pending operation.** `original_setting_status` then reads the campaign slot, but never without the trusted retained confirmation identity: the committed publications must carry exactly the identity the first-writer-wins pin names, the setting the owners hold is rebuilt from their own facts and must hash to it, and the same provenance and value checks as the exact-document readback must pass. A missing, different, malformed or foreign identity is BLOCKED for engineering, never established, and nothing is recreated or adopted.
- **Barrier.** While a setup is unresolved, every other mutating page or chat action stays blocked (the read-only commands and `reconcile` still run). `reconcile_last_action` settles it from the evidence: complete or no effect clears it (a verified complete result settles a lost reply with no further write); a resumable setup stays blocked and names `resume_original_setting`; blocked/unavailable stay blocked. `resume_original_setting` is offered only while the evidence says exactly "confirmed, not whole": it writes the **retained** document to a fresh owner-only file and runs the shipped `establish-locality` for it, which asks the player for a **fresh** confirmation and finishes only the missing stages; it cannot accept replacement data, fabricates no confirmation and authorizes no uncommitted stage silently. A run that ends (a decline, a refusal, an exception, a lost reply, a restart) is settled the same way by owner evidence, never by a status line; a pending record that cannot be cleared keeps the barrier (structural). A legacy `establish-locality` record with no retained document, or a record replaced, malformed or of another campaign, stays blocked for engineering.
- **What the model is told:** an allowlisted out-of-character status (the fixed sentence of the shipped status, never its raw lines except a refusal's bounded diagnostic lines), the player's own retained proposal while an operation still holds it, and the owner-verified readback. No path, secret, challenge or fictional-character perception. "Setting established" is kept distinct from "campaign ready for play", which this change never reports as true.

**Not claimed:** that any real chat client renders the elicitation to a human (see the client-capability limit below); that a model's prose-to-proposal interpretation is faithful or its authored setting good (a separate named-host witness); adaptive World Generation; character creation, activation or physical placement; multiple campaigns or accounts; deployment. The tests are MECHANISM tests (a scripted human, the real service and the real owners, campaigns made only by the shipped `establish-locality`): `tests/integration/chat-original-setting.test.ts` (the proposal → confirmation → owner publications → fresh reopen journey, refusals, whole-confirmation delivery, the recovery matrix, tampered records, locks and residue, races, shutdown, the fallback page and secret canaries), `tests/integration/locality-outcome.test.ts` (the evidence classification at every structural cut) and `tests/unit/chat-setting-confirmation.test.ts`.

## Chat-requested ORIGINAL character (BLUE v5.2-A1, 25 October)

The next small predecessor of conversational onboarding. When the player **actually chooses** to write their own character in their own words, an authenticated chat host can carry those words through the SHIPPED `author-character` path (the I-002 classification, the complete Character Mirror, the retained keep, the I-002 release; then, as a **separate choice**, the IR native/compatibility reading and the Role Registry binding) and the player keeps and places it themselves. The owners, the referee exchange, the Mirror derivation, the keyed publication identities, the retained keep and the verification are the shipped ones; the engine, the contracts, the protocol, the specification registers and the frozen lock are unchanged. The operator package gains one narrow seam (`AuthorCharacterParams.chat`, below) and nothing else.

- **Tools** (player scope only; a referee-only grant cannot list or call them): `propose_original_character` takes the player's words for the fields the shipped console asks (identity; contact surfaces or an explicit no-contact; form; continuity; and optionally exceptions, left-open items and starting possessions), bounded exactly as the console bounds them (each statement trimmed, non-empty, control-free, at most 500 characters; each list at most 12). `revise_original_character` takes typed changes (the console's `set`/`add`/`remove`, without the typed grammar) and applies them to the retained unkept draft all together or not at all. `place_original_character` takes one statement. `original_character_status` is read-only. `resume_original_character` takes no argument. No tool takes a campaign, character or world identity, path, role, permission, secret, grant, release, accepted-item list, binding or "confirmed" flag: the technical identity of the character is the service's (the operator's scenario names it when one is configured, otherwise it is derived from the campaign), and the world and place are the verified original setting's (a missing, unconfirmed, ambiguous or blocked setting refuses). The words a host supplies are untrusted candidate content; nothing is inferred from a profile or memory, completed or improved for the player, and nothing is delegated.
- **Two stages, two runs, two confirmations.** The KEEP run shows the player the whole Mirror and the referee's findings on exactly that candidate; only the human typing back the printed phrase keeps it; then the retained keep, the I-002 decision and the release follow. It **ends there**: the character is released and **not placed**, and the result says so. Placement is never offered because of a keep, never inferred from shared names or an established place, and never silently confirmed: the player's own statement is read by the referee against the committed world and place, shown to the player with the established facts, the exact statement and the findings, and confirmed on its own phrase before the binding. A statement the referee does not support (a portal or arrival, a contradiction, an unclear reading) or one the player does not confirm leaves exactly "released, not bound".
- **Complete, never truncated.** The confirmation is the engine's own lines since the last reply, in order, delivered in as many consecutive parts as the channel needs (a part confirms nothing; withdrawing at any part, or a wrong phrase at the last, confirms nothing). It is judged against what the retained operation says it must contain - every Mirror item of the exact candidate, every finding on it, the closing question and the challenge line; for a placement the facts, the exact statement, the findings and the separate confirmation - and it is **declined, never given,** when this transcript no longer holds it whole from the start of the run. A candidate too large to be sure of showing whole is refused before anything starts.
- **A revision is a new review.** Any change is a new candidate: the earlier question is withdrawn (declined), the referee reads the new whole, and the player keeps the new whole afresh with a new unpredictable phrase. A phrase or an answer to an earlier question confirms nothing later. A kept character is **never replaced** (the keep is first-writer-wins); the words offered after a keep are refused, and the same kept character is not asked again.
- **What is retained before dispatch.** Two adapter-owned, noncanonical files in the operator directory, neither of them consent: the existing pending-operation record gains a `character` identity (the stage, the technical identities, the exact candidate and its contract digest, or the exact placement statement); and `chat-character.<campaign>.json` keeps the player's latest words (the unkept draft in its latest revision, and an offered but unconfirmed placement statement) so an interruption does not lose them. Both are campaign-bound and strictly validated; a malformed, foreign, replaced or digest-mismatched record is blocked for engineering and never adopted, repaired or deleted.
- **Owner evidence about THAT operation** (`apps/phone/src/character-evidence.ts`, read-only: it replays nothing, writes nothing, reclaims no lock and asks nobody): the retained keep (the operator's own record), the release by the shipped `releaseProblems` (the keep's keyed identities, the acceptance that is exactly the Mirror, and the I-002 decision that is the very answer the player saw), the binding by the shipped `bindingProblems` (keyed identities, native explicit insertion, the IR decision), against the verified setting. Distinguished: **no effect** (nothing kept), **kept, not released** (finished by the same keep without a new question), **released, not bound**, **bound** (with an owner-verified readback), **blocked** (a release without a retained keep, a second character, a binding this adapter did not make, a keep for another character, any replaced identity) and **unavailable** (a live or stale lock, an unreadable record: neither done nor not done; the adapter reclaims nothing). A run, a `reconcile_last_action` or a restart settles the barrier only from this evidence about the original operation, never from a status line; a lost acknowledgement at the classification evidence, the release, the placement's referee evidence or the binding converges on the campaign an uninterrupted run makes, with no second release, no second binding and no repeated keep.
- **Resuming.** `resume_original_character` finishes only what the records show was begun: a kept character whose release is incomplete is released from exactly what was kept (**no new keep question**); a retained unkept draft is reviewed again (a fresh question and a fresh keep); a retained placement statement is read again and needs its **own fresh confirmation**. Anything else is refused with its reason.
- **Barrier.** While a character operation is unresolved, every other mutating page or chat action stays blocked; a read-only status never clears it, and a character that exists is not a settled operation. A "kept, not released" state names `resume_original_character`; everything else names `reconcile_last_action` or the operator.
- **Preconditions, checked before the player is asked anything.** The original setting is verified; a referee host is bound for **both** `I002_CLASSIFY` and `IR_NATIVE_COMPATIBILITY` (placement needs the second; neither a host identity nor a grant list is disclosed); the retained words are readable; no other action is unresolved. Provisioning hosts or secrets is not a tool.
- **What the model is told.** The shipped status's fixed sentence, the referee's readings of the player's own words (the same lines the player is shown), and an owner-verified readback in allowlisted terms (the player's kept words, the world and place identifiers, the confirmed statement). No challenge, human reply, keep secret, publication identity, path or sealed task context. "Character kept" and "character placed" stay distinct from "campaign ready for play", which this slice never claims.
- **The operator seam.** `AuthorCharacterParams.chat` selects a stage (`KEEP` or `PLACE`) and supplies the player's draft or presence statement instead of the console questions. In a staged run the Mirror is shown once; a candidate the referee did not close is shown with its findings, offered no phrase and asks nothing; anything but the printed phrase declines (there is no edit grammar to enter); the KEEP run ends at the release; the PLACE run needs a released character. Everything else - the compiled contract, the Mirror, the challenge, the owner operations and the verification - is the console path's own, and the console path is unchanged.

**Not claimed:** that any real chat client renders the elicitation to a human or that a model's prose-to-character interpretation is faithful (see the client-capability limit below); the referee here is a **scripted plumbing host** in the tests, which proves what the engine enforces and nothing about whether an AI understood a sentence; physical custody, a playable scene, time, knowledge, action or perception of the character (a bound character has none of them); general onboarding, adaptive world generation, multi-campaign allocation, deployment, a new grant or secret provisioning. The tests are MECHANISM tests (`tests/integration/chat-original-character.test.ts`, `tests/integration/original-pc-staged.test.ts`, `tests/unit/chat-character-confirmation.test.ts`).

## Authentication (OAuth 2.1)

ChatGPT documents OAuth 2.1 (authorization code + PKCE `S256`) as its only supported authenticated mode (no API keys, no machine-to-machine); the adapter implements exactly that and nothing wider.

- Discovery: `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource`, `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`; a `401` carries `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer resource_metadata=…`.
- Registration: dynamic client registration (`/oauth/register`), public clients only, `https` redirect URIs only (loopback `http` only when the whole service is in `insecure-loopback` development mode).
- Consent (`/oauth/authorize`): a page that states in plain words what is asked. **A scope is granted only by typing that lane's own secret** — `player` by the player secret, `referee` by the (different) referee secret — through the same constant-time, rate-limited check the lane sign-in uses. A client, a registration or a model's wording grants nothing. The player secret never grants `referee`, nor the reverse.
- Tokens: stateless, HMAC-signed, audience-bound to `<public origin>/mcp`, access 1 h, refresh 30 d and single-use (rotated); the signing key derives from **both** lane secrets, so rotating either secret revokes every token. Codes are 5 min and single-use. **Single redemption survives a restart (BLUE R1):** redeeming a code or a refresh token first creates, with exclusive-create semantics, one marker file named by its random id under `<operator dir>/oauth-spent/` (mode 0700/0600); the first redemption wins atomically — across concurrent requests and across restarts — and every later one is `invalid_grant`. If a redemption cannot be recorded it is refused (fail closed); one lost to a crash between recording and answering needs a new consent, which is the honest reconnect. Markers of expired grants are swept. Every token also carries the identity of the one **grant** (consent) it belongs to, unchanged through refreshes. Sessions (`Mcp-Session-Id`) are in memory and bound to the client **and the grant** that initialized them; a restart ends them and the client simply re-initializes.
- The endpoint answers only under its own public host name (DNS-rebinding guard) and refuses any foreign browser `Origin`.

## Confirmation: the human's, never the model's

The shipped commands print an exact proposal and a fresh, subject-bound challenge phrase and accept only that phrase typed to their terminal (anything else declines). The adapter keeps that terminal unchanged and offers exactly **two** places for the _human_ to type it:

1. **The client's own elicitation** (MCP `elicitation/create`, when the client declared the capability at `initialize` and negotiated 2025-06-18). `await_action` carries the question on its own response stream; the engine's proposal text and phrase go to the **human's** client UI; the answer returns to the server directly and is typed into the terminal. It is never a tool argument or result, and `accept` with the wrong phrase, `decline`, `cancel`, a closed connection and silence are all the same non-confirmation.
2. **The confirm page** `/confirm` — a clearly labeled **development fallback**, not accepted as fulfilling in-chat play — for a client that cannot elicit: single-purpose, requires the **player secret** (the assistant has no way to present a session cookie or the secret), shows the same proposal and prompt, and takes the typed phrase once.

What the model is given instead: the engine's report with the challenge phrase **removed** ("the phrase is not shown to the assistant"), no `ask`/`reply` transcript lines and no audit `PROGRESS` lines, a state (`RUNNING`, `AWAITING_CONFIRMATION`, `FINISHED`) and an exit code. `cancel_action` can only decline. A phrase from an earlier proposal does not confirm a later one (each run prints its own).

**Client-capability limit (must be verified live):** the elicitation channel is non-model-controlled only if the client renders it to the _human_ and does not hand it to the model to fill. OpenAI's MCP-server guide does mention elicitation, but neither documentation nor a client's declared capability proves that the Sovereign's actual account/mobile client supplies a native, non-model-controlled human surface; that is **unverified**. A question is also bound to the authorization that asked it (BLUE R2): a response under another grant — even of the same registered client, e.g. a referee-only grant answering a player's question — is refused (`404` for the session, `403` for the question), while a refresh of the same grant and a deliberately dual-scoped host keep working. If the live client can supply neither channel acceptably, the correct result is a STOP for that live route, not a weaker flow.

**Operator diagnostic of the initialize negotiation (24 October).** Each _accepted_ `initialize` writes one line to the service's stderr (the operator's log, never a response, tool result or player view):

```
immortalis-phone: mcp initialize accepted: requested=<2025-06-18|2025-03-26|2024-11-05|OTHER> negotiated=<one of those three> elicitation=<ABSENT|OBJECT|OTHER> form=<true|false|n/a> url=<true|false|n/a> eligible=<true|false>
```

`requested` is the protocol the client asked for when it is one the server supports and `OTHER` otherwise (the server then answers with its latest, `2025-06-18`); `elicitation` is `ABSENT` when the client declared no such capability, `OBJECT` when it declared one as an object (with `form` and `url` stating only whether those members are present) and `OTHER` for any other value (a scalar, `null` or an array); `eligible` is true exactly when the session may be carried on the eliciting transport (an elicitation object **and** the negotiated `2025-06-18`), which is the condition `await_action` then uses. The line is built from that closed whitelist alone. It never contains a client name or version, a capability key or value, a header, a token, a session, grant or client identity, a path or any campaign fact; a refused (unauthorized, wrong-host or malformed) initialize writes nothing. It is not de-duplicated (the service's other operator notes are, once per distinct line), so a repeated identical handshake is visible each time; a missing, throwing or rejecting sink changes nothing about the handshake. **What it is not:** a log line is engineering evidence of what a client _declared_. It does not show that any client renders a confirmation control to the human, and a fresh conversation alone is not evidence of a new grant or session. `tests/integration/chat-initialize-diagnostic.test.ts` covers each capability shape, the older and unsupported protocols, repeated handshakes, refused ingress, sentinel secrets planted in every client-supplied field and header, the real `main.ts` stderr, and the agreement between the logged eligibility and what the transport then does.

## Orchestration without deadlock

Every player operation **returns** (it never waits for the engine to finish): `RUNNING` means the engine is working, perhaps waiting for a referee reading. `await_action` waits for news for at most 25 s — **except** that while it is asking the human it holds the call open for up to **5 minutes** (the elicitation timeout; silence leaves the proposal pending and confirms nothing) — and returns on news, so the same assistant can serve the referee side between two of its own calls (`referee_list_tasks` → `referee_get_task` → `referee_submit_decision`) while the engine waits. Only one action runs at a time; a second is refused. **An uncertain outcome is a persisted, owner-checked block (BLUE R3).** Before any command that can write is dispatched — every player command except the read-only `status`, `locality-status` and `character-status`, whether it is started from the chat or from the phone page — the adapter writes a trusted, campaign-bound pending-operation record (`<operator dir>/chat-pending-operation.<campaign id>.json`, one file per campaign, atomic replace, 0600) naming the command, its closed options, the run and — for an observation — the character, the canonical basis it would be made at and the task and event identities of **every attempt** the shipped command may ask at that basis, all captured beforehand. If that record cannot be written, nothing is dispatched. A definite outcome removes it; an exception, an exit 4 or an engine status of `…OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` / `RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`, or a process that died while it ran (a restart finds the record) leaves it, and then **no other writing command starts, from the chat or the phone page** (the read-only commands, and `reconcile`, still run), the report is `outcome: UNCERTAIN` (never "nothing was confirmed"), and the model is told not to retry or to claim either result. The record is cleared only by **owner-side evidence about that operation**, by class of command:

- **collection operations** (`collect`, `collect-continue`, `collect-finish`, `cease`, `settle-collection-lapse`, `reconcile`): `collection-recovery.ts` captures the operator ledger's immutable identities/digests, active PC, canonical basis/Temporal digest, original collection/act/target, reach task digest/attempt identities and held lineage before dispatch. New choices use the operator's existing trusted `newId` seam, reserving their UID before dispatch while still recording the intent only **after human confirmation**. The shipped operator commands run under their existing session lock; a changed pre-dispatch snapshot refuses execution. Recovery verifies those original identities and owner facts, not a status string or whatever choice is now latest. A missing Temporal registration is not inferred from a prospective extraction object. A partial continuation requires its retained reach decision; a partial ledger operation uses the existing reconciler only when it cannot select an unrelated confirmed choice. A lapse completion remains bound to the captured held lineage. Readback uses a fresh owner-verified reopen after completion. Missing old identities, changed inputs/tasks/lineage, unavailable owners or incomplete records retain uncertainty. An ordinary refusal that follows partial owner writes also retains the barrier; it is not automatically treated as a no-effect outcome. An owner-verified absence can settle an operation without claiming success. Recovery asks neither the human nor the referee again and never invents a confirmation;
- **an observation** (no ledger intent): the recorded PC/basis and all attempt identities locate the retained decision, event, packet and sealed Temporal transaction. A complete observation additionally requires clean Epistemic integrity. No evidence, a retained negative, or complete evidence settles the original operation; none implies arbitrary gameplay success. A partial positive uses the existing `formObservedPacket` owner path directly with the unique original retained decision key. The active PC and canonical basis must still match before completion; recovery never reselects the CLI's current task, asks another choice or rerolls a referee. A later-attempt decision can therefore complete lawfully without changing the operator package. Missing/ambiguous evidence stays blocked;
- **`initialize-time` and `prepare-collection`**: the original document and its SHA-256 pin are retained before dispatch. These two commands derive every publication from that document and campaign alone. Reconciliation reads those exact owner-committed groups: none means no publication of that operation committed; all means complete; a partial preparation remains blocked for the operator to inspect and finish with the identical document. Recovery never asks for another confirmation or repeats the command. Another chronology, setup or campaign cannot settle the original.
- **`establish-locality`** (the ORIGINAL setting, from the chat or the phone page): the exact document, its hash and the setup identity are retained before dispatch and a run is settled only by `localityOutcome` evidence, as described in the section above; a resumable setup is finished only by `resume_original_setting` (the same retained document, a fresh confirmation).
- **anything else** (other setup, authoring and binding commands, or a command not known to the record): no supported readback reconstructs their original typed answers, keep secret or dispatch-time versions. They remain blocked for engineering inspection. Legacy setup records without the document identity also remain blocked.

What the assistant is told is neutral: a blocker never carries the content of a half-formed observation. An unreadable record, an unsupported readback or a record that cannot be cleared are _structural_: the assistant is told to stop and that the operator must intervene (it is not sent round another `reconcile`). An engineer who has inspected the owners may correct or remove the record; every `reconcile` re-reads it, even when an earlier record is cached in memory. Malformed, unreadable or foreign replacements remain blocked and are never overwritten by stale cached state. Removal lifts the adapter barrier; it is not a success receipt or proof that the earlier action did nothing. There is no player or assistant clear-record operation. **Shutdown latches before draining work: no command dispatches after closing begins, including a launch awaiting identity capture. It waits (up to 8 s) for captures, recoveries and running player/referee commands to wind down**, because the command releases the campaign's writer lock only when it has; a hard kill can still strand that lock, which the phone never reclaims (recovery then reports the campaign unavailable until the operator reclaims it). Recovery only presents what the player already confirmed or reads canonical state, so it creates no second consequence; nothing in the adapter retries an action of its own accord or advances canonical time from a wall clock.
