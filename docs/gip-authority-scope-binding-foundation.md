# GIP Authority Scope Binding Backbone Foundation

Fifteenth bounded slice, built on the GIP Office / Authority Locus Backbone Foundation (`docs/gip-office-authority-locus-foundation.md`, thirteenth and fourteenth slices), the GIP Stable Institution Identity Backbone Foundation (`docs/gip-institution-identity-foundation.md`), the Campaign Continuity Foundation (`docs/campaign-package.md`) and the Canonical World Identity & Owner Foundation (`docs/canonical-world-foundation.md`). **One explicitly authored Scope Binding beneath one already-committed Office / Authority Locus** makes the lawful GIP owner (`gip` / `gip_institution_identity`, IM-06) commit **one canonical Scope Binding**; save → close → load → continue preserves the same binding, its parent-office linkage, its authored fields and its provenance; and later lawful subsystems may **read it by exact key without gaining GIP write authority**. In ordinary language: the engine can now remember that **this already-existing office authority is bound to this authored, bounded applicability scope and target condition, with any authored inheritance, exception and conflict references preserved.** It must not — and does not — let anyone infer that the referenced target exists or that anyone belongs to it, that a Governance Instrument exists, that a decision was made or authority exercised, that a rule is valid, active or applicable to a live case, or that anyone knows, accepts, obeys or enforces it.

Governing source (exact pins live in `spec/f027/spec-lock.json` and the owner registry): **WS-007** — the Sovereign-ratified GIP Working Operational Draft v2.1 (frozen F-027 pin `2026-09-24T01:42:24.952Z`) §2.1, §2.2, §5 and §21.1/§21.2, with the GIP Scaling Profile v1.1 §3 (Scope Binding cardinality: Instrument/authority × bounded applicability scope), §4 and §5 (Red-confirmed at pin `2026-09-25T00:59:55.526Z`), the OB-046 closed record and F-017 v0.3 in the lineage. Hard-preflight selection and scope: the fifteenth-slice implementation brief (Notion `3ec20af9-01e0-81c3-b42b-db0a0461cf7a`, pin `2026-10-01T21:03:55.930Z`).

**Not implemented (out of scope, unchanged):** Governance Instruments and any instrument reference; governing-order validity and lifecycle status; live applicability resolution (`applies`, `does_not_apply`, `contested`, `unresolved`), territorial inheritance execution, exception or conflict resolution and promulgation; GIP decision procedures, votes and closure; office appointment, removal, succession, delegation, authority amendment and holder↔decision-body transitions; authority exercise; Actor or Autonomous Collective decisions, consent or promotion; Administrative Reach, implementation, cooperation, enforcement or compliance; institutional KTI consequences (the implemented KTI transfer evidence remains named-recipient scoped); foreign-owner scope state; Epistemic dissemination or awareness; Diplomacy; any new GIP temporal stage, Close Basis, tick, scheduler or polling loop; NPK/UI/host exposure; any RNG, seed, roll, prior, chooser or uncertainty mechanism (GitHub Issue #11 is **still open and untouched**); provider adoption, deployment, migration activation, calibration closure and PF-6/F-028 (**unchanged and blocked**). The acceptance register gains only truthful **partial** evidence (IA-01, IA-02, IA-03, IA-06, IA-22); no row is promoted and no open gate changes.

## Identity, keys, lifecycle and idempotence

- **Exact join.** The binding's `institution_id` and `office_id` are the whole join to its parent office: the admission requires the office at `gip/institution/<institution_id>/office/<office_id>`, pinned in `reads` and `depends_on`. A label never joins, a foreign fact never stands in, and a binding cannot precede its office. The parent office may carry a `holder_ref` or a `decision_body_ref`; the branch changes nothing about the binding, its key, its prerequisite or its write, and the body is never read.
- **One slot per binding of an office.** The key is `institution_id` × `office_id` × `scope_binding_id` (Scaling Profile §3 cardinality: authority × bounded applicability scope; in this slice the authority is specifically an office). A retry, a duplicate construction or a raw-store write reaches the same occupied slot, so no sibling binding exists. Distinct bindings under one office are distinct **only** by their canonical authored `scope_binding_id`: two bindings with identical content under different ids are both kept (nothing is merged or deduplicated), and no binding is ever copied per recipient, resident, place, Population member, actor or transaction.
- **ADVANCING, initial establishment only.** A binding can later lawfully change scope, exception or conflict material, so the kind is `ADVANCING`: the owner's own expected-version chain, not a permanent seal. This slice implements the first creation only; GIP admission refuses every other change (`AMENDMENT_NOT_SUPPORTED`), and a second creation of an occupied key is refused by the store (`VERSION_CONFLICT`). Later GIP work adds owner-defined transitions against the same key — no second ledger or sibling record is needed.
- **Retry converges, conflict fails closed.** Re-presenting the same intent (a lost reply, a restart, a crash at any durable step) is reconciled by identity (RC-16) to the original receipt. A different intent for an established binding — equal or different content — and the same intent with changed content are refused; the committed binding is never rewritten.
- **Authority and text stay at the office.** The binding writes exactly one key. The parent office and the Institution Identity stay byte-for-byte unchanged, and the office's `competence_scope` is never read, parsed, normalised or rewritten by the structured binding.
- **No scan, clock or RNG.** Establishing a binding touches only its parent office key and its own key; reading one touches only its own key; only the diagnostic load-time verifier lists facts, once per open. There is no timer, tick, polling or randomness.

## Interpretations recorded for review

1. **The binding names its parent office by the office's own two identities.** The brief fixes the canonical identity as "parent office authority + `scope_binding_id`" and the office's key is `institution_id` × `office_id`, so the value carries `institution_id` and `office_id` (exactly as the office carries its `institution_id`) and the key and value are cross-checked on admission, on canonical integrity and on load. These are the parent-linkage fields the brief's "exact parent office" requires, not a new scope field, and they are the only fields beyond the §5 minimum.
2. **Source field names — for Blue to confirm.** The brief names the optional fields `exceptions[]` and `conflict_refs[]`; the frozen WS-007 §5 minimum, as read at the pinned artifact, names them `exception_refs[]` and `priority_or_conflict_rule_ref` (a single optional reference). The brief is the controlling construction boundary and this slice follows it, treating `exceptions[]` as the source's exception references and `conflict_refs[]` as an array of opaque conflict/precedence references — all of them carried, never interpreted, ranked or resolved, so nothing semantic depends on which name or cardinality is final. If the source's names or the single-reference shape should govern, it is a rename (and a bounded cardinality narrowing) of two optional fields of one contract, not a change to ownership, keys, admission or persistence. It is flagged here and in the completion report rather than silently resolved.
3. **Authored references are opaque canonical references; classification and conditions are authored text.** `scope_ref` and every exception/conflict reference are the engine's opaque canonical identifier type (like `authority_source_ref`) and nothing else: no existence rule, ontology or resolution. `scope_type` is the source's authored classification field, carried as free authored text (the source's examples are examples, not a closed vocabulary), and `target_condition` and `inheritance_rule` are authored text carried verbatim — no scope taxonomy, condition language or rule language exists, and no interpreter.
4. **Optional means absent or carried.** An optional field the author omits is absent from the persisted value (nothing is defaulted or materialised); an authored empty array is carried as an empty array; `null` and an explicit `undefined` are refused (the latter has no canonical form). No minimum count, uniqueness, ordering or deduplication is imposed on `exceptions[]` or `conflict_refs[]`.
5. **A new governing-rule citation; the office citation is untouched.** The binding kind records `GIP_SCOPE_BINDING_RULE` (same WS-007 artifact, exact native pin and authority lineage as the other GIP kinds; clauses §2.1/§2.2, §5, §21.1/§21.2 and the Scaling Profile §3/§4/§5). `GIP_OFFICE_RULE`, its historical thirteenth-slice companion and the office kind's one `legacyRule` are byte-for-byte unchanged (hashes pinned by tests), so no office is rewritten and the legacy hatch admits no binding. The citation's hash is pinned by a conformance test.
6. **Single facet.** The binding reuses the owner's one registered facet (`gip_institution_identity`, a technical encoding); the kinds are told apart by their exclusive key spaces, as before. No existing GIP kind, fixture or save changes.

These are narrowings, not new rules; each is a candidate for the later GIP work it belongs to.
