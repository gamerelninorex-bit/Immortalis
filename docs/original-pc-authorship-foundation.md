# Original Player-Character Authorship & Native First Activation Foundation

> **OOC DEVELOPMENT / SCENARIO INSTRUMENTATION.** This is an out-of-character local console path by which the actual human at the console authors an ordinary continuing character in their own words, reviews the **complete Character Mirror**, revises the whole until they explicitly keep the last displayed state, and then — as a **separate, second choice** — states and confirms that the character is originally present at an already established world and locus. It is **mechanism only**. It does not prove free-form character generation, physical location, perception, actor willingness, narrated insertion, movement, an autonomous society or First Playable.

Bounded implementation slice (`@immortalis/operator`: `author-character`, `character-status`), composed from two **already implemented** owner operations on `CanonicalCampaign`: `releasePlayerCharacter` (I-002, `pc_existence/existence_contract`) → `bindPlayerCharacter` (Role Registry, `role_registry/player_character_binding`). No engine, contracts, protocol or testkit module changed, the shipped binary (`packages/operator/src/bin.ts`) is byte-for-byte what it was, and no owner extension is registered.

Authority: the BLUE v5 bounded implementation brief "Original PC Authorship & Native First Activation" (native pin `2026-10-10T15:13:41.323Z`) on official `main` `884d09e889d6908e0244a7dbdfb49bbdde060843` (baseline CI run `38059547800`), obligation OB-030 (cross-workstream implementation; overall F-028 stays blocked), under F-027 v1.0 composed with F-027-CD1 (`spec/f027/spec-lock.json`, unchanged), I-002 Draft v2 §§4, 7, 20–21 with the v2.2 Character Mirror amendment and the W3-CP3 mandatory-confirmation-set repair, and Player–World Insertion & Reconciliation Draft v1 §§3, 5–7, 16 with the Canonical Role Registry v1 writer separation. **No source pin, Runtime Contract snapshot, open gate, acceptance row or status changed. ## The sequence

1. **Independent prerequisites** (not this command): World-State already committed the World Profile and then the World Locus (`establish-locality`, PR #60, with its own receipts). The command refuses `WORLD_NOT_ESTABLISHED` / `LOCUS_NOT_ESTABLISHED` before the player is asked anything; a world's existence is never inferred from a later binding.
2. **Authorship, in the player's words, each statement classified by the player.** Six questions in dependency order: who or what you are; how the world can register you (one or more contact surfaces, or an explicit `no-contact me: <why>` — never omitted); your form; what keeps you the same continuing self; what differs from what would normally be expected; what you left open; what you begin with. Free text, no fixed catalogue; nothing is inferred, defaulted, randomized or delegated for the player. **Every statement about the character must open with `me:` (about me alone), typed by the player and never defaulted** (BLUE F61-R1, finding 1): a statement that opens with `world:` or `about-the-world:` is the player saying it concerns something else and is refused (no intake exists, so it is neither stored nor turned into character canon), and an unclassified statement is refused too, because nothing here can tell from the words whether it asserts an office, a follower, an institution or a place. The classification is the player's declaration, **not an adjudication**: the Mirror says so. Every statement is bounded (500 characters, 12 per list), free of control characters, and shown back JSON-quoted.
3. **The whole Mirror.** After the answers, and after **every** revision cycle, the entire Mirror is displayed in one bounded panel (IMPORTANT warning, Identity, Notable traits and abilities, Starting possessions, Still open, About the world, How this character came to be). It is a local fallback panel; the Narrative & Performance Kernel owns final rendering. An incomplete draft (no identity, no contact surface or explicit no-contact, no form, no continuity) is displayed with what is still needed and is **offered no keep phrase at all**.
4. **Revision.** `change`, then lines of a closed grammar (`set identity|embodiment|continuity|contact|no-contact <text>`, `add contact|exception|open|possession <text>`, `remove <list> <n>`), finished by an empty line, are **one revision cycle** applied to the whole draft, all or none (a refused line leaves the character unchanged), after which the complete Mirror is shown again with a **fresh** challenge.
5. **Keep, retained before anything is released.** The player types `keep-character <challenge>` for the **last displayed** state. The token's subject is the exact Existence Contract that will be committed, so a keep cannot cover anything but what was last shown; a phrase of an earlier display, a guess, `no`, free text, or the end of input leaves without a character and writes nothing. **Immediately after the keep and before the release**, the trusted side retains, exclusively and durably, a **keep record** in the owner-only retained-state directory (`character-keep-<hash>.json`, outside the package, the same place and `FsOps` seam as the retained intents and the locality pin): the exact kept contract and a random 256-bit secret that exists nowhere else (BLUE F61-R1, finding 2).
6. **Release (I-002).** `CanonicalCampaign.releasePlayerCharacter` writes `pc_existence/existence_contract` (version 1): every claim `PLAYER-AUTHORED`, no inherited claim, hypothesis, delegation or compilation, and `release.mirror_acceptance.items_displayed_and_accepted` = the ids of the **Mirror of the contract's own content** (`mirrorItems`), which is the same function that produced the panel. The mandatory confirmation set (identity and continuity, embodiment, contact or NO-CONTACT, binding-affecting entry facts, wider-world claims and delegation status) is always among them. The publication identities are keyed digests (HMAC-SHA-256) of the content under the keep's secret.
7. **Placement — a separate, second choice, and only where nothing committed could contradict it.** If the established world or locus carries any committed law departure, insertion-supporting constraint or locus constraint, the command stops with `PLACEMENT_UNRESOLVED` **before asking the player anything** (and, for a character released earlier, leaves it released but unbound): whether a presence statement contradicts such a fact is a semantic judgement (IR §7: "clarify, consume explicit delegation, or leave unresolved") that no producer here can make, and a player's confirmation cannot override an earlier independent owner fact (BLUE F61-R1, finding 1). With none committed, the world and locus are shown in full (premise, designation and the explicit "none"), and the player states, in their own words, that the character is originally present there (native, already there — no arrival, portal, transfer or summoning); an empty line, a decline, or any other answer leaves the character **released but unbound**. The statement is confirmed with `place-character <challenge>` for exactly {character, world, locus, versions, statement}.
8. **Bind (Role Registry).** `CanonicalCampaign.bindPlayerCharacter` writes `role_registry/player_character_binding` on `NATIVE_ALREADY_THERE` / `EXPLICIT_PLAYER_AUTHORED_FACT` under `role.player` + `permission.player_choice.commit`, with the statement preserved verbatim and the exact PC, world and locus versions pinned. One receipt, one journal group.
9. **Fresh reopen.** The package is closed and opened again through the ordinary owner-verified open; the contract (identities, items, provenance) and the binding (pins, statement, identities) are read back and compared. Only then is `CHARACTER_BOUND` reported.

## Identity, convergence and fail-closed

Publication identities are derived from the exact confirmed content (a hash of the campaign identity and the contract; of the campaign, character, world, locus, pinned versions and statement), with no clock, counter or random value (`operator-pc:<id>:release|bind`, `operator-pc-intent:…`, `operator-pc-cause:…`). So:

- **A release this command did not keep is never bound by it, however exactly it matches.** Everything in a published release — its content, its Mirror items, its publication names — is derivable from public contract content, so the canonical fact alone cannot show who accepted it. A release is recognised only with (a) the retained keep record, whose contract equals the committed one, and (b) publication identities keyed by that record's secret, which no public content reproduces and which appears nowhere in canon. A mechanically identical contract published by another caller through `releasePlayerCharacter` — under the identities of the first release of this slice, a digest of the contract alone, or a keyed digest under a guessed secret — is `CHARACTER_CONFLICT` with no prompt and no write, as is one whose accepted items are not exactly the Mirror of its content, or that carries inherited or hypothetical claims or a compilation. A caller-submitted `RELEASED` status or `items_displayed_and_accepted` array is not proof of the preceding human review. The binding identities are keyed the same way. **Residual:** whoever can write the owner-only retained-state directory is the operator (the local OS user who owns the package); this is the same local trust boundary as every other operator command, not a stronger identity, and the record is the trusted side's memory, not canon or authority. Losing the retained-state directory leaves a released character unrecognised (it is reported as a conflict, never adopted).
- **A campaign has one such character.** Only the Existence Contract kind (`pc_existence`/`existence_contract`) counts as a character: other lawful facets of the PC owner — a received capability basis, a capability close — are facts about a character that already exists and never make another one (BLUE F61-R1, finding 3). A second Existence Contract, another kept character identity, and any operative binding this command did not make for this character or at another locus, are `CHARACTER_CONFLICT` / `BINDING_CONFLICT`; nothing is replaced (the owners' writes are create-only).
- **Retry/reopen.** The same command again reads what the campaign holds: a character kept but not yet released (an interruption between the retained keep and the release) is released exactly as kept, without asking again; a character released earlier resumes at placement only (`RESUMING`; no authoring prompt, no second keep, no second release — the kept contract is read back, so the character cannot change on replay); a binding that committed is verified and reported without asking again; nothing is duplicated. A kill before the keep leaves no character (the player is asked again).
- **Released but unbound is reported as exactly that** (`CHARACTER_RELEASED_UNBOUND`). The two writes are two owners' separate publications; there is no cross-owner atomic rollback, none is invented, and a failed or declined insertion never shows as a binding.
- **Unknown outcomes** (a lost reply, an unexpected failure) are `CHARACTER_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never a success or a clean refusal; the session refuses further commands until closed, and the rerun reconciles by the original identities. A kill before the release leaves no character (the keep is not retained: the player is asked again); a kill after it leaves the released character, and the stale package lock is reclaimed only by the explicit `--reclaim-stale-lock`.

## Refusals (all fail closed at the existing admission/receipt boundary and leave canon unchanged)

Absent, declined, guessed, stale or earlier-display keep; a changed or incomplete character at keep time; forged accepted items or a caller-built release; unclassified statements and `world:` / `about-the-world:` claims in authorship, revision or placement (an unprefixed office or follower claim included); a committed world or locus constraint (`PLACEMENT_UNRESOLVED`); a mechanically identical release with no retained keep; a second Existence Contract (while a capability basis of the PC is not one); control characters and over-long statements; another campaign identity; a campaign directory others may write; a missing world, a missing locus, or a locus of another world; an empty, declined or stale placement; an inferred placement (the statement and its own confirmation are the only source); a portal, summoning, delegated or strong-implication insertion, a persona authority, or a wrong character identity (the owners' boundary, exercised directly in the tests); an already operative binding; a different character on replay; interruption at any point.

## Not claimed

- Free-form or universal character generation, any model-hosted interpretation or a parser of free text. The only boundary on world-owned, office, follower or institution claims inside a character statement is the player's own `me:` classification, shown as their attestation: **a statement the player falsely classifies as about themselves alone is still recorded as theirs.** Judging that a sentence asserts a world-owned fact is the I-002 compiler's classification (`role.gm`, I-002 §2.1/§6 step 6) and the receiving owners' authority test; **that producer does not exist in this build and is the indispensable dependency for closing this completely** (BLUE F61-R1, finding 1). Safe inheritance is not computed: `safely_inherited` is empty and everything recorded is `PLAYER-AUTHORED`.
- **Physical position or locus, perception, knowledge, action, movement, time, narration, actor willingness, autonomous society, or First Playable.** The binding is an Insertion/Role Registry record. **No first-ACTIVATION physical custody is established** (`PcPhysicalLocusCustody` is derived from World-State actor coupling on handover and still rejects a new first ACTIVATION); no role-binding referent is reinterpreted as a current physical owner, and `pcCurrentLocus` stays undefined.
- Semantic compatibility of the player's statement with the world's free-text facts. The committed constraint and law-departure lists make the placement `PLACEMENT_UNRESOLVED` (nothing is bound) because no adjudicator exists; the broad premise, canon and designation are shown to the player and are **not** adjudicated (a premise that excludes the described kind of character is not detected). A mechanical conflict (a locus that is not the world's, a missing prerequisite) is the owner's refusal.
- Human presence, a login, a remote identity or a player identity namespace: the local operator is the OS user who owns the package directory, as for every other operator command, and "the player" is whoever can type to its console.
- Provider or recovery acceptance (IA-07 stays BLOCKED). **PF-6/F-028 remains BLOCKED / NOT ACTIVATED; IA/V rows are unpromoted; no gate status changed.**
