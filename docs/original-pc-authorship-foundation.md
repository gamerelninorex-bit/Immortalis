# Original Player-Character Authorship & Native First Activation Foundation

> **OOC DEVELOPMENT / SCENARIO INSTRUMENTATION.** This is an out-of-character local console path by which the actual human at the console authors an ordinary continuing character in their own words, reviews the **complete Character Mirror** together with an **AI referee host's findings on exactly that text**, revises the whole until they explicitly keep the last displayed state, and then — as a **separate, second choice** — states and confirms that the character is originally present at an already established world and locus, which the same referee reads against the committed world. It is **mechanism only**. It does not prove free-form character generation at any quality, physical location, perception, actor willingness, narrated insertion, movement, an autonomous society or First Playable.

Bounded implementation slice (`@immortalis/operator`: `author-character`, `character-status`, `referee-bind-host`, and the host-callable `immortalis-referee` stdio MCP server), composed from two **already implemented** owner operations on `CanonicalCampaign` — `releasePlayerCharacter` (I-002, `pc_existence/existence_contract`) → `bindPlayerCharacter` (Role Registry, `role_registry/player_character_binding`) — and, since BLUE B61-A3, one infrastructure-only provenance owner, `referee_adjudication_kernel / referee_provenance` (see [`docs/ai-referee-host-foundation.md`](ai-referee-host-foundation.md)), whose retained decisions those two operations cite. The shipped binary (`packages/operator/src/bin.ts`) is unchanged and no owner extension is registered.

## The sequence

1. **Independent prerequisites** (not this command): World-State already committed the World Profile and then the World Locus (`establish-locality`, PR #60, with its own receipts); an AI referee host is bound to the campaign (`referee-bind-host`). The command refuses `WORLD_NOT_ESTABLISHED` / `LOCUS_NOT_ESTABLISHED` / `REFEREE_UNAVAILABLE` before the player is asked anything; a world's existence is never inferred from a later binding.
2. **Authorship, in the player's words.** Six questions in dependency order: who or what you are; how the world can register you (one or more contact surfaces, or an explicit `no-contact <why>` — never omitted); your form; what keeps you the same continuing self; what differs from what would normally be expected; what you left open; what you begin with. Free text, no fixed catalogue and **no classification prefix to type**: nothing is inferred, defaulted, randomized or delegated for the player. Every statement is bounded (500 characters, 12 per list), free of control characters, and shown back JSON-quoted.
3. **The referee reads the exact candidate.** For a complete draft, the engine builds a bounded task (the candidate statements, each with its slot) and publishes it for the connected AI host. The host answers statement by statement (the player's own claim, a bounded starting possession, an intentionally open branch — or an origin memory, a setting premise, an office or standing, an independent actor's stance, a world-state assertion, a setting-law proposal, a mixed statement, or an ambiguous one), quoting the player's words. The engine **validates** the answer (closed vocabulary, every statement decided once, quotations verbatim) and **derives** the verdict itself: `CLOSED`, `BLOCKED` or `CLARIFY`. Nothing is closed because a host said so.
4. **The whole Mirror, with the findings.** After the answers, and after **every** revision cycle, the entire Mirror is displayed in one bounded panel together with the referee's findings on exactly that candidate: which statements it accepts as the player's own and, for each it does not, why, the words it points at, the part that is the player's own (for a mixed statement; never applied for them), and any one question for the player. An incomplete draft, or one the referee did not close, is displayed but **offered no keep phrase at all**.
5. **Revision.** `change`, then lines of a closed grammar (`set identity|embodiment|continuity|contact|no-contact <text>`, `add contact|exception|open|possession <text>`, `remove <list> <n>`), finished by an empty line, are **one revision cycle** applied to the whole draft, all or none, after which the changed candidate is classified again and shown again with a **fresh** challenge.
6. **Keep, retained before anything is released.** Only when the engine derived `CLOSED`, the player types `keep-character <challenge>` for the **last displayed** state. The token's subject is the exact Existence Contract **and** the referee question it was closed under; a phrase of an earlier display, a guess, `no`, free text, or the end of input leaves without a character and writes nothing. **Immediately after the keep and before the release**, the trusted side retains, exclusively and durably, a **keep record** in the owner-only retained-state directory (`character-keep-<hash>.json`, outside the package): the exact kept contract, the referee's answer the player kept on, and a random 256-bit secret that exists nowhere else (BLUE F61-R1, finding 2).
7. **Referee evidence, then release (I-002).** The classification is committed once, create-only, as a `referee_decision` (the engine applies `role.gm` + `permission.rule.adjudicate` from its own trusted binding — never from the host's answer or the player's text). `CanonicalCampaign.releasePlayerCharacter` then writes `pc_existence/existence_contract` (version 1) citing that decision (`qualified_by`): every claim `PLAYER-AUTHORED`, no inherited claim, hypothesis, delegation or compilation, and `release.mirror_acceptance.items_displayed_and_accepted` = the ids of the **Mirror of the contract's own content**. The engine admission and integrity refuse a release that cites a decision which is not a `CLOSED` classification of exactly that contract's statements. The publication identities are keyed digests (HMAC-SHA-256) of the content under the keep's secret.
8. **Placement — a separate, second choice, read by the referee against the committed world.** The world and locus are shown in full; the player states, in their own words, that the character is originally present there; an empty line or a decline leaves the character **released but unbound**. The engine builds the IR task **only from committed facts** (the world's premise, its reference/causal basis and established canon, its law departures and insertion constraints, the locus designation and constraints, the accepted character and the player's statement) and the host assesses every delivered fact and reads the statement's mode. The verdict is derived: `BIND_ELIGIBLE` only for an explicit native, already-there reading with no contradicted and no unclear fact; `UNSUPPORTED_BRIDGE` (arrival, portal, transfer, summoning, other), `WORLD_CONFLICT` (a committed fact contradicts it; **neither side is weakened for convenience**) or `UNRESOLVED` (the referee cannot tell and asks one question) bind nothing. The player's `place-character <challenge>` confirmation is for exactly {character, world, locus, versions, statement, referee question}. There is no blanket refusal on a non-empty constraint list: a constraint that bears on nothing relevant does not block.
9. **Bind (Role Registry).** The IR decision is committed once, then `CanonicalCampaign.bindPlayerCharacter` writes `role_registry/player_character_binding` on `NATIVE_ALREADY_THERE` / `EXPLICIT_PLAYER_AUTHORED_FACT` under `role.player` + `permission.player_choice.commit`, with the statement preserved verbatim, the exact PC, world and locus versions pinned, and the qualifying `BIND_ELIGIBLE` decision cited. One receipt, one journal group each.
10. **Fresh reopen.** The package is closed and opened again through the ordinary owner-verified open; the contract, the binding and both referee decisions are read back and compared. Only then is `CHARACTER_BOUND` reported.

## Identity, convergence and fail-closed

Publication identities are keyed digests under the keep's secret; the referee task identity is a hash of the exact question (campaign, attempt, candidate or committed-fact versions, statement), so the same question is the same task on every run. So:

- **A release this command did not keep is never bound by it, however exactly it matches.** Everything in a published release is derivable from public contract content, so the canonical fact alone cannot show who accepted it. A release is recognised only with (a) the retained keep record, whose contract equals the committed one, (b) publication identities keyed by that record's secret, and (c) a qualifying referee decision cited by the release (a legacy release without that evidence is **not** retroactively attested). A mechanically identical contract published by another caller is `CHARACTER_CONFLICT` with no prompt and no write. A caller-submitted `RELEASED` status or `items_displayed_and_accepted` array is not proof of the preceding human review or of any classification. **Residual:** whoever can write the owner-only retained-state directory is the operator (the local OS user who owns the package); this is the same local trust boundary as every other operator command, not a stronger identity.
- **A campaign has one such character.** Only the Existence Contract kind counts as a character: other lawful facets of the PC owner never make another one (BLUE F61-R1, finding 3). A second Existence Contract, another kept character identity, and any operative binding this command did not make for this character or at another locus, are `CHARACTER_CONFLICT` / `BINDING_CONFLICT`; nothing is replaced (the owners' writes are create-only).
- **Retry/reopen.** The same command again reads what the campaign holds: a character kept but not yet released (an interruption between the retained keep and the release, or between the referee evidence and the release) is released exactly as kept, with the referee's answer the player kept on, without asking again; a character released earlier resumes at placement only; a binding that committed is verified and reported without asking again; nothing is duplicated.
- **A silent host is `REFEREE_PENDING`, never a verdict.** The operator stops with no canonical change; the same question (same task identity) is found again on the next run, with any answer the host has meanwhile given. An answer that is not vouched for (keyed proof of the bound host, for this task and its one-time nonce, over the exact answer) or that the engine refuses is **set aside, never reused**, and the question is asked again as the next attempt.
- **Released but unbound is reported as exactly that** (`CHARACTER_RELEASED_UNBOUND`). The writes are separate owners' publications; there is no cross-owner atomic rollback, none is invented, and a failed or declined insertion never shows as a binding.
- **Unknown outcomes** (a lost reply, an unexpected failure) are `CHARACTER_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never a success or a clean refusal; the session refuses further commands until closed, and the rerun reconciles by the original identities. The stale package lock is reclaimed only by the explicit `--reclaim-stale-lock`.

## Refusals (all fail closed at the existing admission/receipt boundary and leave canon unchanged)

Absent, declined, guessed, stale or earlier-display keep; a changed or incomplete character at keep time; a character the referee did not close (any statement that is not the player's own, mixed or ambiguous); forged accepted items or a caller-built release; an answer from an unbound host, with a forged, crossed or altered proof, for another task, missing a statement, or quoting words that are not there; a release or binding that cites a decision which does not qualify, or none; a mechanically identical release with no retained keep; a second Existence Contract (while a capability basis of the PC is not one); control characters and over-long statements; another campaign identity; a campaign directory others may write; a missing world, a missing locus, or a locus of another world; an empty, declined or stale placement; an inferred placement (the statement and its own confirmation are the only source); an arrival, portal, summoning or transfer reading, a committed fact the referee finds contradicted, or one it cannot judge; a portal, summoning, delegated or strong-implication insertion, a persona authority, or a wrong character identity (the owners' boundary, exercised directly in the tests); an already operative binding; a different character on replay; interruption at any point.

## Not claimed

- **The semantic quality of the AI referee is NOT verified by the automated tests.** Every automated host in the test suite is a scripted policy that the test wrote; those tests prove the plumbing (the engine asks, vouches for the host, validates the answer against the delivered text, derives the verdict itself, refuses what it did not derive, recovers from interruption) and nothing about whether any model understood any sentence. Semantic acceptance requires a live exercise with a real AI host, reviewed by a person; see the referee document for what was and was not observed. The engine validates an answer's form and its grounding in the delivered words; it does not and cannot check that the reading is right.
- Free-form or universal character generation at any quality, or a parser of free text in the engine: the engine contains none. There is no fixed fantasy catalogue and no word list.
- **Physical position or locus, perception, knowledge, action, movement, time, narration, actor willingness, autonomous society, or First Playable.** The binding is an Insertion/Role Registry record. **No first-ACTIVATION physical custody is established** (`PcPhysicalLocusCustody` is derived from World-State actor coupling on handover and still rejects a new first ACTIVATION); no role-binding referent is reinterpreted as a current physical owner, and `pcCurrentLocus` stays undefined.
- Human presence, a login, a remote identity or a player identity namespace: the local operator is the OS user who owns the package directory, as for every other operator command, and "the player" is whoever can type to its console. A host's `claimed_model` is a self-reported label retained as provenance; it is not an identity proof.
